# Product Notes: Samaj Data Front-End Revamp

**Core Objective:** Rethink the front-end experience for "Samaj Data" to better motivate different types of users to take civic action. 

**What is Samaj Data?** 
A repository of hyper-local, geographical data for civic issues. It maps problems down to a street-level resolution and provides a toolkit of hyperlocal solutions successfully implemented by others.

---

### 1. User Personas (The Civic Behavior Matrix)
To understand our users, we divide them into a 2x2 matrix based on their awareness and actions regarding civic issues:

*   **Unconsciously Wrong (UW):** They don't know if their actions are right or wrong. *Example: Thinking carrying/using single-use plastic bags is perfectly fine.*
*   **Consciously Wrong (CW):** They know their actions are harmful but continue to do them anyway without caring. *Example: Knowing burning plastic is bad, but doing it anyway.*
*   **Unconsciously Right (UR):** They do the right things purely out of habit, cultural upbringing, or local practices. They don't explicitly know the positive civic impact, it's just ingrained behavior.
*   **Consciously Right (CR):** They actively know the right actions to take, understand the causes and impacts, and deliberately execute them.

---

### 2. User Journey Strategy: Migration vs. Solution
The ultimate goal of the platform is to push people toward taking action. The strategy changes based on which quadrant the user is in.

*   **Target for the "Wrongs" (UW & CW) $\rightarrow$ MIGRATION.** We need to migrate their mindset.
*   **Target for the "Rights" (UR & CR) $\rightarrow$ SOLUTION.** We need to push them to take physical action.

**Tactics for each quadrant:**

*   **For Unconsciously Wrong (UW):** 
    *   *Tactic:* Don't just show them they are wrong; rephrase it to show the *potential* of doing it right. Use surprise and awe.
    *   *Migration Path:* UW $\rightarrow$ CW or UW $\rightarrow$ CR.
*   **For Consciously Wrong (CW):** 
    *   *Tactic:* Since they might be fed up or lack hope, show them the potential combined with the **lowest effort levers**. Give them a tiny task that yields a monumental amount of dopamine or a sense of pride.
    *   *Migration Path:* CW $\rightarrow$ CR.
*   **For Unconsciously Right (UR):**
    *   *Tactic:* Celebrate and anchor them. Show them the platform data so they realize, *"Look what you've been doing without even knowing!"* Push them to do more. 
    *   *Migration Path:* UR $\rightarrow$ CR.
*   **For Consciously Right (CR):**
    *   *Tactic:* Celebrate them heavily. These are our **high-effort levers**. They are primed to do the heavy lifting for monumental impact.
    *   *Target:* Direct them straight to building/executing solutions.

---

### 3. Taxonomy of User Actions (The Solution Toolkit)
When users are ready to act, we need to present them with options ranked by **Effort vs. Impact** (and the resulting "pride" factor). 

Here are the actions users can take through Samaj Data, scaling from low to high effort:

1.  **Changing Habits:** Modifying personal, day-to-day behavior.
2.  **Awareness:** Spreading information to close groups/friends about local problems and how they impact the community.
3.  **Reporting:** Acting as data points. Verifying if a known issue/solution still exists, or submitting completely new local data.
4.  **Complaints/Advocacy:** Filling out official forms, signing petitions, or running local campaigns.
5.  **On-Ground Follow-up:** Visiting a previously fixed site (e.g., a repaired pothole) to verify if the long-term solution held up and is still in good condition.
6.  **On-Ground Solution:** Hands-on, physical intervention (e.g., going out to actually fill a pothole or clean up a garbage blackspot).
7.  **Building Solutions (High Effort):** Creating sustainable systems. This includes building an organization, developing tech solutions, or sparking a community movement around the issue.

*** 
*Note-taker's tip: When designing the UI, you can use the actions in Section 3 to map directly to the strategies in Section 2. For example, serve "Reporting" or "Awareness" to your CW users (low effort/high pride), and serve "Building Solutions" or "On-Ground Solutions" to your CR users (high effort levers).*
